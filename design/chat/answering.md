# Answering

A topic of [Chat](../chat.md): the capsule and its field, a send through the queue, what comes back on your bubble, `--timeout 30`, a false `not confirmed`, why `waiting` takes no text.

- **The capsule** (`src/routes/agent/Composer.svelte`, how it looks in Look › Components): one rounded box that holds, in this order, `+` (`Attach image`), the keys' icon (`Keys`), the field and a round `Send`, with the buttons at its foot, so as the text grows the capsule rises and the buttons stay under the thumb. Over it, in the composer, the question, the row of keys and the images' thumbnails, in that order. The user asked for it on 2026-11-10 after trying 029 on the iPhone, as the composers of Claude, WhatsApp and Messages are; 016 to 039 had stacked the keys, the chips, the field and a `Send` with its label, which took most of a phone's height while the keys are needed mostly while the agent asks. On a phone of 375 pt the text keeps about 211 px beside three buttons of 44 px, so no fourth button fits there.
- **The field** is a `TextArea` without a frame of its own (`bare`, Look › Forms), since the capsule draws the border and the focus ring, that grows with its text up to a maximum and then scrolls. Return adds a line and only `Send` sends: on a phone's keyboard Return is the only way to break a line, and a message sent by mistake cannot be taken back. `Send` rests while there is neither text nor an image waiting, while it sends, while an image is being attached and while the agent is `waiting`.
- **Cmd+Return or Ctrl+Return sends** (062, a hardware keyboard on a tablet; `sendsOn` and `sendOnKey` in `src/lib/keys.ts`): it sends when `Send` would and otherwise does nothing, and adds no line either way; Return alone still adds a line, outside a composition. A `keydown` cannot tell a hardware Return from the on-screen one, and a send cannot be taken back, so Return stays a line break, as above. Rejected: Return sends with a hardware keyboard and Shift+Return breaks the line, as Messages on a Mac, for the reason just given.
- **Through the queue**: `send --pane P --enter --timeout 30 -` by `Queue::ask`, with the text as `Stdin::Text`, so it keeps its order with the keys (Chat › Keys). On standard input, a text that starts with `-` is not read as an option, and the text never shows in the argv cornercase logs. No message nor log holds it.
- **What comes back**: `Send` empties the field and takes the images waiting at once, and the thread shows what went as your bubble, `Sending…` (Chat › The thread). cornercase's answer turns it `Sent`, or `Not sent:` with the reason, since it answers something the user did: cornercase's refusal without its `Error:` (`waiting`, a dialog, a poll, `resuming`, `not confirmed`, a held send that ran out of time), `nothing was sent` for a job the queue skipped, `it may have been sent, but no answer came back` for one that lost its answer, and `no machine is connected` without a queue. cornercase's texts are written for scripts (`answer with cornercase keys`, `or with --force`) and are shown as they are: rewriting them would guess at upstream's words.
  - **Said by the bubble, not a toast**: the reason stays beside the text it is about, while a toast of 8 s would leave with it and leave the text nowhere. The keys keep their toasts (Chat › Keys), and so does attaching an image.
  - **`Retry` and `Edit`**: `Retry` sends the same text again with `Send`'s rules, pasting first the images it took that are not on the machine yet (Chat › Attaching an image › When a paste fails), and rests when `Send` would (another send on its way, an image being attached, `waiting`). `Edit` puts the text back in the field, before what was typed since and apart from it by a blank line, and takes the bubble away. A failed send gives back the images it took, before those attached meanwhile.
  - **One send at a time**: `Send` is busy while a bubble is `Sending…`, and the field still takes text.
  - **The field keeps the keyboard**: `Send` cancels its `mousedown` (`preventDefault`), so the field it empties keeps the focus and the keyboard stays open for the next words (041). Without it, a tap on `Send` took the focus and closed the keyboard. WebKit on iOS moves the focus in the mouse events it makes up after a touch, not on `pointerdown`: cancelling `pointerdown`, as 041's plan said, still closed the keyboard in the simulator, as it did for the terminal's row (Terminal › Typing › The row of keys), and cancelling `touchend`, as that row does, would drop the click that sends. A tap with the field unfocused still sends.
  - **The text does not come back**: 029 reported, on the user's iPhone, the text sent showing again in the field, maybe iOS' predictive text committing a pending word after the field emptied. In the simulator with the software keyboard, predictive text and autocorrection (Chat › Checked live), the text left with the bubble in the same tap and nothing came back, so nothing else changed: closing the composition before emptying the field (a blur and a focus, which also close and reopen the keyboard, or emptying on `compositionend`), the fix held ready for it, was not needed. The iPhone is checked again by the user.
- **Your send shows at once**: 016 showed nothing until `agents-changed` turned the header `working` and the message was read again with its event, and the field just emptied. On the user's iPhone, on 2026-11-09, that read as no feedback at all: nobody could tell whether the message had gone, nor whether the agent was thinking or stuck. So the text shows as soon as it is tapped, `Sending…` until cornercase answers. It is what the user sent, not a guess at the agent's state, which still comes only from `agents-changed` and the reads.
- **`--timeout 30`**: upstream holds a send while someone types on the machine (its `held-sends.md`). With 30 s, a held send ends in `nothing was sent` rather than waiting for ever. Meanwhile the queue is busy, so a key tapped behind it waits up to 30 s (Chat › Keys). Accepted. Rejected: 15 s, the least over the 3 s of the Enter and the 10 s of the confirmation, which leaves a held send almost no time; no timeout, which would let a held send stop the queue for good.
- **A false `not confirmed`**: a Codex that works keeps a prompt sent meanwhile in memory and records it after its next tool call, so upstream extends its 10 s confirmation up to `--timeout` (its `confirming-sends.md`). A tool call longer than 30 s then fails with `not confirmed` though Codex takes the prompt later. Accepted. The bubble says `Not sent` with that reason, which says it may not have been submitted, so the user can look before `Retry` sends it twice. Any `not confirmed` may have reached the agent for the same reason.
- **`waiting` takes no text**: upstream refuses `send` to an agent that asks a question, a permission or a choice, which is answered with a key (Chat › Keys) or an option (Chat › The question). There, `Send` rests under the agent's question, or, until its rows come, under the note `<agent> is asking you something on its screen; answer with the keys.`, and the text typed stays, as gpui's `unready` does. Rejected: sending anyway and showing the refusal, a round trip for nothing.
