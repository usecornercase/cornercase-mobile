# Typing

A topic of [Terminal](../terminal.md): the phone keyboard and the row of keys turned into the contract's events, paste, the height with the keyboard, swiping and what was checked live.

Code: `crates/core/src/cli/input.rs` (the events), `src-tauri/src/commands/terminal/input.rs` (the commands), `src/lib/terminal/` (`keys.ts`, `typing.ts`, `Typist.svelte`, `KeyRow.svelte`, `row.ts`, `ctrl.svelte.ts`, `paste.ts`, `sending.ts`, `keyboard.svelte.ts`, `swipe.ts`, `touch.ts`), `src/routes/terminal/Terminal.svelte`.

- **The events are the contract's**: `InputEvent` is `key` (`code`, `mods`, `kind`), `text`, `paste` or `mouse` (`kind`, `button`, `x`, `y`, `mods`), serialized exactly as upstream's `input` reads them (`src/control/terminal.rs` of cornercase-terminal): `mods` left out when empty, `kind` when it is a press, `button` when it is the left one, mouse kinds in kebab case. The key names are upstream's (`term::keys::NAMED`). They reach TypeScript through tauri-specta, so the WebView builds them with the core's own types. `terminal_input(pane, events)` pushes each event on the typist of the active `Store`'s queue (`input --follow --json -`, Connection › The ordered queue) and `terminal_scroll(pane, rows_up)` on its coalesced `scroll`; without a `Store` or a queue both answer `not_connected`. Both are synchronous: they only hand the queue its lines. The queue still takes a JSON `Value`, so the command turns the typed events into one, and the queue's logic did not change. That conversion cannot fail for these types, but `serde_json` returns a `Result` and `unwrap` is denied, so a failure would answer a JSON error rather than panic; it has no test, as nothing can make it fail. Rejected: a typed `Queue::input`, a change to the queue and its typist for no behaviour. `Sending` (`sending.ts`) sends to the pane the screen shows (`terminal-changed.pane`), nothing once the tab closed or before a pane showed, and turns a refused event or scroll into a toast, without trying again; the typist and the key row are disabled once the tab closed.
- **The pane's modes reach the WebView** (`Update.modes`, `terminal-changed.modes`, `PaneState.modes`), as small as the cursor and sent whole each time, also on a patch that changes the modes and no row: the swipe needs them to choose what to send.
- **The phone keyboard types into a hidden `textarea`** (`Typist.svelte`): 1 px, transparent, without pointer events, at the screen's top left, with `autocapitalize`, `autocorrect` and `autocomplete` off and `spellcheck` false, and a 16 px font, as below that iOS zooms the page on focus (Look › Field); it takes the focus with `preventScroll`. `Typing` (`typing.ts`, no DOM) turns its `beforeinput` into events and cancels it, so the field never keeps what was typed: `insertText` and `insertReplacementText` (a prediction, dictation) become `text`, `insertLineBreak` and `insertParagraph` `enter`, `deleteContentBackward` `backspace`, `deleteWordBackward` `backspace` with `alt` (readline's `backward-kill-word`: a held delete on iOS speeds up to whole words, and may come as that alone, which swallowed would stop the deleting midway; `ctrl+w` was rejected, as it deletes up to the last space rather than the last word), `insertFromPaste` (the edit menu's Paste) a `paste`. During a composition (an IME) nothing is sent and nothing cancelled; `compositionend` sends its text once. The field always goes back to a sentinel, a single space with the caret after it, so a Backspace on a field that looks empty still has something to delete and still fires. `keydown` takes the keys of a physical keyboard (arrows, Esc, Tab, Enter, Backspace, Delete, Home, End, Page Up, Page Down and F1 to F12, the names of upstream's `term::keys::NAMED`, with Ctrl, Alt and Shift, and `Ctrl` with a character); one with Cmd is left to the system. Enter and Backspace come both as a `keydown` and as a `beforeinput`, so the `beforeinput` that follows a `keydown` of the same key is swallowed and the key goes once; the key is forgotten on its `keyup`, so a `keydown` whose `beforeinput` never comes cannot swallow a later one, such as the line break of a dictation. Rejected: `keydown` alone, which carries no text for a prediction, dictation or a composition; reading the field's value after each `input` and diffing it, which keeps the text and guesses at what a replacement replaced; the task's plan B, a visible field that sends a whole line, not needed so far (an IME is still to be checked on an iPhone).
- **A tap focuses it in the `click`** (`src/lib/terminal/Terminal.svelte`): the `pointerup` of a finger that moved less than 10 px only marks a tap (`Tap` in `touch.ts`), and the `click` that follows calls `focus`, still inside the gesture, so iOS opens its keyboard; a `pointercancel` drops the mark, and a swipe or a pinch is no tap. Found in the simulator (2026-11-09, iOS 27.2): focusing in the `pointerup` gave the field the focus, and the `click` iOS makes up for the same touch then gave it back to the body, so the keyboard never opened. A tap only focuses: it sends no mouse click to a program that reads the mouse (the task's P2). Rejected: a click on the cell as well, which in Claude Code moves things on a touch meant to open the keyboard; clicks wait for a task of their own.
- **A hardware keyboard types without a tap** (062, `design/look/wide.md` › Hardware keyboard): the screen listens to `keydown` on the document, and a key that nothing else owns goes to `Typist.take` (`strays` in `keyboard.svelte.ts`, next to the selector `Keyboard` already used for the field being edited: aimed at the page or at the screen off any field or control, and not handled before), which focuses the hidden field and sends what `Typing.stray` makes of it: what `physical()` names (arrows, Esc, Tab, Enter, F-keys, Ctrl+key), with the sticky Ctrl as everywhere, or a single printable character without Cmd or Ctrl as `text`, the event cancelled so nothing else takes it; composition, a key the input method processes (229), Cmd shortcuts, bare modifiers and dead keys stay the system's. The next keys reach the field itself. Only a hardware keyboard fires a `keydown` with no field focused, so this never opens the on-screen keyboard. A focused button, link, select or summary keeps its keys, so `Back`, the switches, the keys' row and, in two panes, the side and the close popover answer Enter, Space and Tab as before; the terminal takes them once the focus is on the page or the grid. Until the review of 062 it also took them from focused buttons, which broke activating them from a keyboard and sent the popover's `Esc` to the agent. Rejected: focusing the field when the screen opens, which opens the on-screen keyboard on every phone.
- **The row of keys** (`KeyRow.svelte`, `ROW` in `keys.ts`): `Esc`, `Tab`, `Ctrl`, `↑ ↓ ← →`, `|`, `~`, `/`, `-` and `Paste`, each at least 44 px tall and 52 px wide (Look), in a grid of as many columns as fit (`repeat(auto-fit, minmax(52px, 1fr))`), so on a phone the row breaks into two lines of six or seven and on a wider screen keeps one; 52 px holds `Ctrl` in the 15 px mono font with 8 px each side, under the terminal at the bottom of the screen, and above the keyboard while it is open. The symbols go as `text`. A key acts on `pointerup`, and its `touchend` is cancelled, which drops the made-up click and with it the change of focus, so the keyboard stays open. A `click` with no pointer behind it (`detail` 0: Enter or Space on a hardware keyboard, VoiceOver, Switch Control) acts too, as no `pointerup` came before it; a pointer's click has a `detail` of 1 or more and is left alone, so a key never goes twice. Found in the simulator: the planned `pointerdown` with `preventDefault` closed the keyboard on every key all the same, and a touch's `pointerdown` is not a user activation in WebKit, so the clipboard refused Paste (`NotAllowedError`). `Paste` is drawn as Lucide's `clipboard-paste`, named `Paste` for VoiceOver: as a word it did not fit the iPhone Air's 420 pt and was cut at its right edge. The arrows are Lucide's `arrow-up`, `arrow-down`, `arrow-left` and `arrow-right`, named `Up`, `Down`, `Left` and `Right`, as in the chat's row. Found on an iPhone (task 022's review): twelve keys sharing one line (`flex: 1 1 0`, `min-width: 0`) were about 27 px each at 375 pt, and `Ctrl`, `Esc` and `Tab` ran out of their buttons. Rejected: a wider key for Paste, which squeezes the others; one line that scrolls sideways, which hides `Paste` and the symbols behind a swipe that the terminal's own sideways pan also wants. Checked in the iPhone Air simulator (2026-11-09, iOS 27.2) with the row alone at 375, 420 and 440 pt: two lines (six and six, then seven and five), every key 58 px or wider at 375 pt, and no label wider than its button.
- **A sticky Ctrl** (`StickyCtrl` in `keys.ts`, `ShownCtrl` in `ctrl.svelte.ts` for the key's look): `Ctrl` stays marked (`aria-pressed`) until the next key, of the row or of the keyboard, which it goes with and is then released; a second tap disarms it. A character with it becomes the key of that character with `ctrl` (`c` → `{"key":{"code":"c","mods":["ctrl"]}}`); a row key gets `ctrl` added; when the keyboard inserts several characters at once (a prediction), only the first takes it and the rest go as `text`. A `paste` goes as it is and still releases it.
- **Paste** (`paste.ts`, the task's P4): `navigator.clipboard.readText()`, on which iOS shows its own Paste callout; a tap on it sends the text as one `paste`, and cornercase brackets it when the program asked for it. An empty clipboard or a refusal is a toast, with nothing sent. Rejected: `tauri-plugin-clipboard-manager`, a dependency the WebView's own API made needless in dev; it comes back only if the release build (`tauri://`) refuses the clipboard.
- **The height with the keyboard** (`keyboard.svelte.ts`): while anything covers the screen (`visualViewport.height` short of the tallest `innerHeight` seen at the current width at all, `covered`), the screen takes the visual viewport's height and its `offsetTop` (iOS lays the keyboard over the page and may scroll it on focus) and drops its bottom safe area, so the row sits on the keyboard, or on the bar of shortcuts alone, about 55 to 70 pt, that iOS shows with a hardware keyboard. The keyboard counts as open only when more than 100 px are covered, which that bar is not, nor a rounding of the viewport's height. Whenever the scroller's height changes, the grid is scrolled to its bottom, so on a grid taller than the space left its last row sits right above the row of keys. A size `fit` asks while the keyboard is open is dropped (`whileClosed`): the size is measured without the keyboard, so opening or closing it never calls `terminal_open` again nor restarts the `surface`, and the machine is never resized. The bar of a hardware keyboard is no keyboard there: the space it leaves is asked for, which restarts the `surface` once as it shows and once as it goes, and never resizes the machine either. Limit: a grid shorter than the space left, as a 50-row pane at the fitted width, stays at the top with a gap under it.
- **On Android the page shrinks with the keyboard** (032): `interactive-widget=resizes-content` in the viewport, as upstream's `design/browser-access.md`, and `MainActivity.kt` pads the WebView by the keyboard's insets (Architecture › Android), so `innerHeight` and `visualViewport.height` both drop to what is left above it and `offsetTop` stays 0. Comparing the two, as on iOS, would never call it open (they differ only by a rounding), and `whileClosed` would let each opening and closing ask for a new size, restarting the `surface` and, with Fit to this phone, resizing the machine (seen in the emulator before the fix: four `surface` runs for one opening and closing). So `seen` keeps the tallest `innerHeight` seen at the current width (`tallest`) and measures what is covered against it, on both platforms: on iOS the page does not shrink, so the tallest is `innerHeight` and nothing changes; a turn of the screen changes the width and starts again from the new height. `Keyboard` keeps it only while a field has the focus (`textarea`, `input` or `contenteditable`, as only a field opens the keyboard): with none, the page's height is the tallest, so a page that shrinks at the same width with no keyboard (a split screen, a window over the app, a folding phone) is a smaller screen, which the terminal fits and Fit to this phone asks the machine for, and not a keyboard left open for ever (task 032's review). The screen's `top` and `height` need nothing else on Android, and the page is not left scrolled once the keyboard closes. Limits: a terminal screen that opens while a keyboard is still up takes the short page as its tallest until that keyboard closes (opening it from the chat's field closes the chat's keyboard first, checked), then asks for the full size once as it closes, which restarts the `surface` once; and a split screen that shrinks while the terminal's field has the focus is read as the keyboard until the field loses it or the width changes. Rejected: `navigator.virtualKeyboard` with `overlaysContent`, the opposite of `resizes-content` and missing from some WebViews; working out the height in JavaScript from `env(keyboard-inset-height)`, when Android documents applying the insets to the view.
- **Android keyboards type one character at a time** (GBoard, checked live): each letter comes as a `keydown` with `keyCode` 229 (`Unidentified`, the keyboard is processing it) followed by a `beforeinput` `insertText` that is no composition, so the machine gets it at once, not word by word, and no change to `Typing` was needed for it (the task's P2, a). A `keydown` 229 always passes untouched and is not remembered as the pressed key, even when its `key` names one (`Enter` while the keyboard still processes it): the `beforeinput` that follows decides, so a key never goes twice nor is swallowed. Backspace and Enter on GBoard are plain `keydown`s (8 and 13) with no `beforeinput`, one `backspace` or `enter` each. GBoard shows its row of suggestions in the hidden field all the same, though the WebView already asks for `NO_SUGGESTIONS` (`inputType` `MULTI_LINE | NO_SUGGESTIONS | WEB_EDIT_TEXT`), and it does not autocorrect; a tap on a suggestion inserts only what is missing, so it breaks nothing. Rejected: `inputmode` or `enterkeyhint` on the field, which do not govern that flag and would be a risk for iOS for nothing seen; plan B's compose field as Android's default, not needed while GBoard does not compose. Compositions (gesture typing, voice, accents) keep iOS's rule, sent once at `compositionend`, and are covered by the tests with an Android keyboard's sequences, not seen live.
- **The keyboard's accessory bar stays** (the task's P3): WKWebView's ‹ › ✓ bar over the keyboard is part of what `visualViewport` leaves out, so the screen sits above it, and its ✓ closes the keyboard, after which the screen takes the whole height again. Rejected: a Swift plugin that hides it, as Orca does because the bar lifts its page twice, which this screen did not see.
- **Swiping** (`swipe.ts`, `touch.ts`, the task's P1): a finger that moves 10 px or more from where it touched is a swipe for the rest of the touch. Its vertical movement becomes lines at the current zoom (the movement over the line's height), keeping the rest for the next move as gpui's `scroll_rest` does, scrolling naturally: a finger going down brings the history up. A grid taller than the screen, once zoomed, first moves itself in the DOM towards its edge, and the rest scrolls the history; a fitted grid at the base (`pinned` in `touch.ts`, also within half a pixel over it, as a pinch back out can stop at 13.2 px rather than at 13) is held where it is and the whole movement goes to the history or the program (Swiping a fitted grid). What the lines send follows the pane's modes, as gpui's `wheel_event` and cornercase's `scroll` (which refuses the alternate screen and a program that reads the mouse): to a program that reads the mouse, a `scroll-up` or `scroll-down` per line at the cell under the finger; else on the alternate screen with `alternate_scroll`, an arrow per line; else, off the alternate screen, `scroll` by rows; on the alternate screen without it, nothing. One move sends at most 25 lines. A second finger spoils the swipe and the tap, and is the pinch's. The grid's place and the scroller's room are measured only once the finger slides: reading them forces a layout, and on every move of a pinch, as the font size changes, that was a layout per move for nothing. Rejected: the history always, with the zoomed grid moving only sideways or held at its bottom, which would undo the vertical scrolling of a zoomed grid that 020 brought, in either mode. A touch that starts within 20 px of the screen's left edge is first the swipe back's (Look › Navigation): while it waits or holds the finger (`edge`), the grid takes none of its moves, and a lift once it is claimed is no tap; a still tap there is a tap, so the first column still takes it.
- **Swiping a fitted grid** (043): on the user's iPhone (029) a vertical swipe went wrong in a fitted terminal. In the iPhone Air simulator (2026-11-10, iOS 27.2, cornercase 0.13.19) it had three causes. First, under the grid's `white-space: pre`, the space between the rows and the cursor drew one more line under the last row, so a 39-row grid was 1.3 em taller than its rows and overflowed the box they fill by 12 px: each move spent that false room moving the grid before the history, and after each patch WebKit put the scroller back at its bottom (not `anchor`, nor scroll anchoring: `overflow-anchor: none` changed nothing; why is not known), so a 300 px swipe brought 6 or 7 rows instead of 17, the screen shaking 12 px at each. Second, the zoom shared with the passive mode (Terminal › The grid › Zoom). Third, with the keyboard open, the fitted grid, sized without it (The height with the keyboard), is taller than the space left, so a swipe first moved about 350 px of grid, some 20 rows, before the history moved, and the same on the way back. Only fitted, as only there the rows fill the box exactly, so one more line overflows it: passive at its smallest zoom the grid is shorter than the box and the line fits, and zoomed there was real room already, where 12 px more go unseen. The fix: no `white-space: pre` on the grid (Terminal › The grid › Drawing it), the zoom apart per mode, and a fitted grid at the base held at its bottom, its rows above the keyboard coming into view as the history scrolls; after it, 300 px moved 17 rows and the swipe back returned to the same ones. Claude Code (the alternate screen and a mouse that reads any motion) gets the wheel, 17 notches for 300 px, and never `scroll`, so no `conflict`; how far a notch moves its transcript is its own. Rejected: dropping the space from the markup (a later edit brings it back, and nothing shows it), taking a line off what `roomIn` measures (room for a grid that is not there), holding every fitted grid, a pinched one too (it undoes the vertical scrolling of a zoomed grid), and momentum (not a cause). Not checked: over Tailscale, where each line waits for its patch (To check on an iPhone). Checked live after the fix (2026-11-10, as in Fitted by default › Checked live fitted, with XCUITest's touches): in the shell, 300 px moved 17 rows each way, and with the keyboard open 200 px moved 11 each way, stopping at the bottom; `less` at the base got 17 `↓` for 300 px and 17 `↑` back, with the grid not moving in the DOM; zoomed to 26 px, the grid first moved itself in the DOM, as before. A pinch in, the sideways pan of the zoomed grid, the tap that opens the keyboard and the edge swipe back worked in both modes. Not checked there: pinching back to the base, as XCUITest's pinch out did not zoom out, and any pinch with the keyboard open, as XCUITest pinches at the window's centre, which is then on the keyboard (To check on an iPhone).
- **Checked live** (2026-11-09, iPhone Air simulator, iOS 27.2, Tauri 2.12.5 in `ios dev`, cornercase 0.13.19 through the bridge, with real touches): through the board, the agent's screen and `Terminal`, on a shell (a `zsh` named `claude` to show on the board) and on Claude Code 2.1.319 working. In the shell: a prompt of about 160 characters in order, Backspace (on the sentinel too), one line per Enter, `↑ ↓` through the history, `← ←` and typing in the middle, `Tab` completing a folder, `Esc`, `Ctrl-C` stopping `cat` and `sleep`, `Ctrl-U`, Paste through the system's callout, and a swipe down bringing the history up and back. In Claude Code: a long prompt, Backspace, `←` and typing in the middle, and Enter sending it. The PTY stayed at the Mac's 139×50 with the keyboard open or closed, and opening and closing it again and again opened no `surface`. The three findings above (the tap's focus, the row closing the keyboard, Paste refused) were fixed then. Not checked: `Tab`, `Esc`, `Ctrl`, Paste and swiping in the agent, a physical keyboard (the simulator's was off), the heights in between while the keyboard slides, and the release build's clipboard. The bridge logged two `surface` runs as the first screen opened, before any key, which did not happen again when it was opened again; not explained.
- **Checked live on Android** (2026-11-10, arm64 emulator, Android 17, API 37, WebView 145.0.7632.218, GBoard 17.2.2, a debug APK against a cornercase 0.13.19 of its own behind the bridge, with a `bash` named `claude` in the pane): with GBoard open the screen is 578 px tall of 914, the row of keys sits right on the keyboard and the grid's last row above the row, and closing it (back) gives the whole height back with nothing scrolled. Opening and closing it four times opened no `surface`; with Fit to this phone on, the only `surface`s were the ones its switch asks for. A turn of the screen with the keyboard open restarts the `surface` once for the new size. Typed: letters one by one, a word with a typo sent as it was, a suggestion tapped, Backspace on the sentinel, Enter running the line once; and with the emulator's hardware keys, arrows through the history and Ctrl-C.
